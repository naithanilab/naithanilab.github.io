Lab Reading List

Reading and synthesizing information is essential to succed in graduate school. Below is the recommended reading list to start developing common knowledge base as a productive lab group member.

Statistics and Experimental Design

Books

  • Staton, Ben. Introduction to R for Natural Resource Scientists. n.d. Accessed January 30, 2026.

  • Hacking, I. (2006) The emergence of probability: A philosophical study of early ideas about probability, induction and statistical inference. Cambridge University Press. 2006

  • Hilborn, R. and Mangel, R. (1997) The Ecological Detectives. Princeton University Press. March 6, 1997.

  • Isaaks, E.H. and Srivastava R.M. (1989) An Introduction to Applied Geostatistics. Oxford University Press, 1989.

Papers

  • Hurlbert, S. H. Pseudoreplication and the design of ecological field experiments. Ecol. Monogr. 54, 187–211 (1984).

  • Allen, C. R., D. G. Angeler, G. S. Cumming, C. Folke, D. Twidwell, and D. R. Uden. 2016. Quantifying spatial resilience. Journal of Applied Ecology 53:625–635.

Ecology

Courchamp, Franck, and Corey J. A. Bradshaw. “100 Articles Every Ecologist Should Read.” Nature Ecology & Evolution 2, no. 2 (2018): 395–401. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-017-0370-9.

And some more:

  • Real, Leslie A., and James H. Brown, eds. Foundations of Ecology: Classic Papers with Commentaries. University of Chicago Press, 1991.

  • Wiens, John A., Michael R. Moss, Monica G. Turner, and David Mladenoff, eds. Foundation Papers in Landscape Ecology. Columbia University Press, 2006.
